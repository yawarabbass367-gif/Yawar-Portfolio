# Content Strategy

A strong portfolio is a narrative that builds trust. Each section should move visitors one step closer to contact—matching how recruiters and hiring managers typically evaluate portfolios: first impression → credibility → evidence → breadth → action.

Related: [PRD.md](./PRD.md) · [INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md](./INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)

## Trust ladder

```
Home → About → Case Studies → Projects → Contact (persistent CTA)
```

| Step | Page / surface | Role in the narrative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Home | Capture attention and establish expertise |
| 2 | About | Build credibility and explain the approach |
| 3 | Case Studies | Demonstrate product thinking and problem-solving |
| 4 | Projects | Showcase breadth and versatility |
| 5 | Persistent Contact CTA | Make it easy to reach out |

---

## 1. Home

**Intent:** Capture attention and establish Product Designer / Fintech · AI · Enterprise expertise within seconds.

**Primary message:** I design enterprise, fintech, and AI products that simplify complex workflows.

**Proof needed:** Clear positioning, credible industries/companies, 2–3 featured case studies with outcomes, a concise process signal.

**CTA:** View case studies · Contact / schedule conversation

### Homepage: three questions in seconds

| Question | Answer the hero must deliver |
| --- | --- |
| Who are you? | Product Designer (`{{FULL_NAME}}`) |
| What do you design? | Enterprise software, fintech platforms, and AI-powered digital products |
| Why trust you? | Strategic product design and research-driven decisions that solve business problems |

**Copy guidance**

- Lead with role and specialization, not a long bio.
- Brand statement may sit under the hero headline.
- Avoid trendy slogans; prefer precise, confident language.
- Featured case studies should lead with problem + outcome, not only visuals.

---

## 2. About

**Intent:** Build credibility and explain how the designer thinks and works.

**Primary message:** Product thinking + user-centered process + enterprise experience.

**Proof needed:** Professional introduction, philosophy, approach, experience timeline, skills, tools, industries.

**CTA:** Explore case studies · Contact

**Copy guidance**

- Tell a coherent career/product story, not a résumé dump.
- Connect values (simplicity, scalability, trust) to how work is done.
- Keep tools secondary to judgment and outcomes.

---

## 3. Case Studies

**Intent:** Demonstrate product thinking and problem-solving with depth.

**Primary message:** I understand products, not just screens—and I can simplify complex enterprise workflows.

**Proof needed:** Problem framing, business goals, users, research, decisions, constraints, process artifacts, results, lessons.

**CTA:** Next case study · Contact about similar work

**Copy guidance**

- Prioritize *why* decisions were made over decorative UI walkthroughs.
- Quantify results when possible; otherwise state qualitative business/user impact clearly.
- Follow [templates/CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE.md](./templates/CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE.md).

---

## 4. Projects

**Intent:** Show breadth and versatility beyond flagship studies.

**Primary message:** Experience across industries and product types.

**Proof needed:** Thumbnail, name, industry, platform, role, brief description, technologies if relevant.

**CTA:** Open project detail · View related case study (if any) · Contact

**Copy guidance**

- Keep descriptions short; link to a full case study when one exists.
- Emphasize role and domain over tool lists.

---

## 5. Persistent Contact CTA

**Intent:** Convert interest into interviews, hires, freelance discussions, or opportunities.

**Primary message:** Easy next step—talk about product design work.

**Proof needed:** Clear contact paths with filled placeholders:

- Email: `{{EMAIL}}`
- Calendar: `{{CALENDAR_URL}}`
- LinkedIn: `{{LINKEDIN_URL}}`

**CTA options (use consistently sitewide)**

- Get in touch
- Schedule a conversation
- Discuss a project

**Placement**

- Primary CTA in global nav and/or header
- End-of-page CTA on Home, About, Case Studies, Projects
- Footer contact links

---

## Voice and tone

- Premium, confident, intelligent—never loud or salesy
- Product and business language over decoration-first design speak
- Specific over generic (“fintech onboarding workflow” beats “beautiful experiences”)
- No filler marketing fluff, emoji clusters, or trend-chasing copy

## Key beliefs visitors should leave with

- This designer understands products, not just screens
- They can simplify complex enterprise workflows
- They have fintech and AI experience
- They think strategically
- They care about users and business outcomes
